# Maharashtra Traffic Rules and Regulations

## Overview
Maharashtra follows the Motor Vehicles Act, 2019 with state-specific implementations and penalties. This document provides information about traffic rules specific to Maharashtra state.

## Key Traffic Rules in Maharashtra

### 1. Licensing Requirements
- **Minimum Age**: 18 years for cars and motorcycles (without gear), 20 years for commercial vehicles
- **Learning License**: Valid for 6 months, must display 'L' board
- **Permanent License**: Valid for 20 years (renewable) or until age 50, whichever is later
- **Medical Certificate**: Required for commercial vehicle licenses

### 2. Vehicle Registration
- Registration mandatory within 30 days of purchase
- Maharashtra registration format: MH-XX-YY-ZZZZ (e.g., MH-01-AB-1234)
- RC book must be carried while driving
- Vehicle fitness certificate required after 15 years for personal vehicles

### 3. Helmet and Safety Equipment
- ISI marked helmets mandatory for riders and pillion passengers
- Seat belts mandatory for driver and front passenger
- Children under 14 must not sit in front seat
- Child restraint systems recommended for young children

### 4. Speed Limits in Maharashtra
- **Urban areas**: 40-50 km/h
- **Rural areas**: 60-80 km/h
- **National Highways**: 100 km/h (cars), 80 km/h (two-wheelers)
- **Mumbai city limits**: 50 km/h (40 km/h in some areas)

### 5. Drunk Driving Limits
- Blood Alcohol Content (BAC) limit: 30mg per 100ml of blood
- Zero tolerance for commercial vehicle drivers
- Breathalyzer test mandatory if suspected

### 6. Pollution Norms
- PUC (Pollution Under Control) certificate mandatory
- Valid for 6 months for petrol vehicles, 3 months for diesel
- BS-VI norms applicable for new vehicles
- Older vehicles may be phased out based on age

### 7. Special Rules for Two-Wheelers
- Pillion riding allowed only with proper footrest
- Only one pillion passenger permitted
- Helmet mandatory for both rider and passenger
- High-beam lights prohibited in city limits
- Modified silencers/exhausts are illegal

### 8. No Horn Zones
- Hospitals, schools, courts, and religious places
- Silence zones clearly marked with signboards
- Fine applicable for honking in these areas

### 9. Mobile Phone Usage
- Using mobile phone while driving is strictly prohibited
- Hands-free devices also discouraged while driving
- Heavy penalties for violation

### 10. Lane Discipline
- Keep left, overtake from right
- Use indicators while changing lanes
- Don't cross continuous white/yellow lines
- Follow lane markings on highways

## Mumbai-Specific Regulations

### Peak Hour Restrictions
- Heavy vehicles restricted during peak hours (7-11 AM, 5-10 PM)
- Some areas have complete ban on heavy vehicles

### One-Way Streets
- Extensive one-way system in South Mumbai
- Clearly marked with signboards
- Follow direction arrows on roads

### No-Entry Zones
- Specific time-based no-entry restrictions
- Check signboards for timing
- Alternative routes usually provided

### Parking Regulations
- Pay-and-park facilities in commercial areas
- Towing policy active in no-parking zones
- Multi-level parking facilities available

## Vehicle Insurance Requirements

### Third-Party Insurance
- Mandatory for all vehicles
- Covers damage to third-party property and injury
- Annual renewal required

### Comprehensive Insurance
- Optional but recommended
- Covers own damage and third-party liability
- Higher premium but better protection

### No-Claim Bonus (NCB)
- Discount for claim-free years
- Up to 50% discount after 5 years
- Transferable between policies

## Traffic Enforcement

### E-Challans
- Automated traffic violation detection
- CCTV cameras at major junctions
- SMS notification to vehicle owner
- Online payment option available

### Manual Challans
- Issued by traffic police officers
- Receipt provided immediately
- Payment within 60 days to avoid court summons

### Vehicle Impounding
- May be impounded for serious violations
- Release requires fine payment and documentation
- Storage charges applicable

## Road Safety Tips

### For Car Drivers
1. Always wear seat belt
2. Maintain safe following distance
3. Check mirrors before lane changes
4. Use indicators properly
5. Don't drive when drowsy or under influence
6. Keep emergency kit in vehicle
7. Service vehicle regularly

### For Two-Wheeler Riders
1. Always wear ISI helmet
2. Use proper riding gear
3. Be extra cautious at intersections
4. Avoid lane splitting in heavy traffic
5. Check tire pressure regularly
6. Use both brakes properly
7. Be visible (proper lights and reflectors)

### For Pedestrians
1. Use zebra crossings and foot over-bridges
2. Look both ways before crossing
3. Don't use mobile phone while crossing
4. Be alert to turning vehicles
5. Wear reflective clothing at night

## Legal Rights During Traffic Stop

### You Have the Right To:
1. Ask for officer's ID and name
2. Know the reason for being stopped
3. Receive a copy of the challan
4. Politely question the violation
5. Pay fine online later (for e-challan)
6. Contest the challan in court
7. Video record the interaction (in public space)

### You Must:
1. Stop when signaled by traffic police
2. Provide valid documents when asked
3. Be polite and cooperative
4. Not argue aggressively
5. Not offer or accept bribes
6. Follow officer's instructions for safety

## Contesting a Challan

### Steps to Contest
1. Note down all details of the incident
2. Collect evidence (photos, videos, witnesses)
3. File objection within 60 days
4. Attend court hearing if required
5. Present evidence and arguments
6. Court will make final decision

### Valid Grounds for Contesting
- Wrong vehicle number on challan
- You were not driving at that time
- Vehicle was stolen
- Technical error in speed camera
- Medical emergency situation
- Traffic signal malfunction

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What if I don't have documents while driving?
A: You may be issued a challan. However, you can submit documents at the police station within 24-48 hours to avoid fine.

### Q: Can police seize my vehicle?
A: Yes, for serious violations like drunk driving, driving without license, or dangerous driving.

### Q: How to check pending challans?
A: Visit mahatrafficechallan.gov.in and enter your vehicle number.

### Q: What if I lost my driving license?
A: Apply for duplicate license at nearest RTO with required documents and fee.

### Q: Can I drive with a learning license alone?
A: No, a licensed driver must accompany you.

### Q: How long do I have to pay the fine?
A: Usually 60 days. After that, court summons may be issued.
